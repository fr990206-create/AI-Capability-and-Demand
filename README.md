## Core Research Arguments

### 1. Human Demand for Labor Substitution

Beyond productivity improvements and cost reductions, a fundamental driver of AI demand is the human desire to avoid labor itself.

### 2. Nonlinear Demand Growth

When AI transitions from partially assisting humans to reliably completing entire tasks autonomously, demand may experience nonlinear growth.

### 3. From AI Capabilities to Revenue

AI capabilities determine the scope of potential automation. Task coverage, adoption rates, compute consumption, and pricing determine how this potential translates into revenue.

### 4. Sustainability of AI Infrastructure Investment

The economic sustainability of AI capital expenditure ultimately depends on genuine and recurring end-user demand.

### 5. Beyond Diminishing Marginal Returns

Advances in AI capabilities may continuously unlock new markets and applications. Diminishing returns within individual tasks do not necessarily imply diminishing economic returns across the entire AI industry.

### 6. The Fiber-Optic Bubble and AI

Unlike the fiber-optic investment boom around 2000, AI technological progress may simultaneously expand computing supply and create new demand patterns through autonomous agents and bots.
